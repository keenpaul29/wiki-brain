## Developer Time Management and Productivity

### Deep Work (Cal Newport)

Complex debugging, architecture design, and learning new tech are all Deep Work. 1 hour deep > 5 hours shallow. Four scheduling philosophies:

- **Monastic:** Block all shallow work (Knuth lives without email)
- **Bimodal:** Alternate long monastic stretches with shallow periods
- **Rhythmic:** Same block every day (e.g. 6-9 AM)
- **Journalistic:** Snatch deep work whenever possible (requires practice)

Protection strategies: notifications off, physical separation (meeting room/cafe), start ritual (coffee, music), end ritual (declare done for today).

### GTD for Developers

**Five steps:** Capture (move everything out of head) → Clarify (decide what it means) → Organize (sort by category) → Reflect (regular review) → Engage (pick what to do now).

**Two-minute rule:** if it takes under 2 minutes, do it now. Applies to short emails, tiny PR reviews, adding code comments.

**Context groups:** @computer, @phone, @office, @waiting, @someday.

**Weekly review:** 30-60 minutes. Drain inbox, review active projects, pick top 3 for next week, update Someday list.

### Calendar Blocking

Assign every hour to a block. Decide upfront what happens when.

**Buffer rules:** 15 min between meetings, 30 min between cognitive blocks, 1.5x time estimate for unfamiliar work.

**Sample maker schedule:**
- 09:00-11:30 Deep Work: core coding
- 11:30-12:00 Slack/email triage
- 13:00-14:00 Meetings (clustered in afternoon)
- 14:00-15:00 PR review
- 15:00-17:00 Deep Work: second block

### Time-Boxing vs Pomodoro

**Time-boxing:** assign a strict time limit to each task. "Review this PR → 30 min. If stuck for 2 hours, ask a colleague."

**Modified Pomodoro:** Standard 25/5 is too short for coding. Better: 45-60 min focus + 10 min break, or 90 min (ultradian rhythm) + 20 min break. Match technique to task: complex coding = 90 min block, simple PR review = 25 min Pomodoro.

### Maker vs Manager Schedule

**Manager schedule:** one-hour slots, meetings as default.
**Maker schedule:** 2-4 half-day blocks. A single meeting destroys a block.

Developers are makers. Cluster meetings in afternoon. Declare no-meeting mornings. Replace sync with async (recorded video, rolling docs).

### Slack, Email, Meeting Triage

**Slack hygiene:** use threads, finish thought in one message, silence during focus blocks, open at set times only (10:00, 13:00, 16:00).

**Email triage:** Delete → Reply (<2min) → Delegate → Defer → Archive. Time cap: 15 min morning, 10 min noon, 10 min evening.

**Meeting triage:** accept only if you're a decider, pre-read exists, purpose is clear, no other slot available.

### Burnout Prevention

**Early signs:** hard to get out of bed, no interest in meetings, dread of Slack, disproportionate anger at small bugs, weekends no longer recover you, sleep drops, exercise stops.

**Prevention:** 7-8 hours sleep, 30+ min exercise daily, real lunch break away from coding, kill Slack in evening, use all PTO, one stretch of 1+ weeks off annually.

**12-Item Productivity Checklist:**
- [ ] Deep Work blocks: 10+ hours/week
- [ ] No Meeting Day: one per week
- [ ] Inbox Zero: once a week
- [ ] Slack time limit: set quiet hours
- [ ] Weekly Review: 30 minutes
- [ ] 3 MITs: pick at start of each day
- [ ] Meeting pre-read: share day before
- [ ] Exercise 3x/week: 50+ minutes
- [ ] Sleep 7+ hours
- [ ] Sabbatical plan: 2+ weeks every 3-5 years
- [ ] 1-on-1 notes: every time
- [ ] Burnout check: self-check monthly

### Remote Work Traps

Blurred boundaries, isolation, less movement. Solutions: morning ritual (clothes, coffee, walk), evening ritual (close laptop, clock out step), workspace split (don't work from bedroom), leave house 1x/week.
