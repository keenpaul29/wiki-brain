## Structured Engineering Hiring

Structured interviewing uses uniform methods to assess all candidates for the same role: same questions, same scoring rubric, same qualifications. Research shows 2x the predictive validity of unstructured interviews.

### Why Structured Beats Unstructured

- Unstructured interviews suffer from confirmation bias — interviewers form snap judgments and hunt for confirming evidence
- Structured interviews predict job performance significantly better in academic research spanning 20+ years
- Google found: interviewers save ~40 minutes per interview using pre-made rubrics, candidates are 35% happier with the process even when rejected
- Structured assessment tools (behaviorally-anchored rating scales) are more predictive than unstructured conversations

### Four Components

1. **Vetted, high-quality questions** relevant to the specific role
2. **Comprehensive feedback recording** so evaluators can review responses
3. **Standardized rubrics** defining what outstanding/solid/borderline/poor looks like
4. **Interviewer training and calibration** for consistent assessments

### Defining Hiring Attributes

Before any questions, define what success looks like at 6 months, 1 year, 2 years. Three categories for engineering roles:

**Role-Related Knowledge:** Does the candidate have the technical know-how? Use behavioral questions ("Tell me about a time...") to validate claims.

**Problem Solving:** How does the candidate think through complex scenarios? Use hypothetical questions ("Imagine if...") to see how they handle ambiguity.

**Leadership:** For ICs, look for initiative and collaboration. For managers, look for team empowerment and vision.

### Behavioral vs Hypothetical Questions

- **Behavioral:** "Tell me about a time..." — reveals past behavior patterns, validates resume claims
- **Hypothetical:** "What would you do if..." — tests thinking under novel situations both types in the same interview

### Scoring with Rubrics

Define what poor, borderline, solid, and outstanding answers cover for each attribute. Every interview must be scored against the same scale. Rubrics that are never updated stop reflecting the role.

### Interview Process Design

- **2 rounds** for most technical roles (screen + deep), **3 rounds** for staff+
- **Target:** 8-12 business days from first contact to offer
- **AI-allowed coding:** in 2026, prohibiting AI tools is increasingly unjustifiable — evaluate how candidates use them
- **Score first, discuss later:** everyone writes assessment before debrief meeting to prevent anchoring
- **Same-day debrief** for speed — losing candidates to process delays is the most common hiring failure

### Bias Mitigation

- Standardize questions and rubrics so variation is not attributable to candidate quality
- Define "culture fit" specifically as behaviors, not "feels like one of us"
- Diverse interview panels surface wider signal range
- Track diversity data at drop-off points — audit for barriers
- Avoid unstructured portions contaminating scored sections

### Debrief Process

1. Everyone writes assessment before meeting (no reading others)
2. Each shares with specific evidence
3. Discussion focuses on areas of disagreement
4. Final decision with documented rationale

### Metrics to Track

- Offer acceptance rate (target >70%)
- Time-to-hire by stage
- Pass rate by stage (above 60% at screen means bar is too low)
- 90-day performance correlation with interview scores

### Common Pitfalls

- Starting without a defined scorecard
- Over-indexing on algorithm puzzles — system design shows higher correlation with real-world senior performance
- Too many rounds (more than 3 loses strong candidates)
- Rubrics without interviewer training — scoring consistency drives validity, not question consistency
