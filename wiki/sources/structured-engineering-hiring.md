# Structured Engineering Hiring

## Summary

Google's guide to structured interviewing for engineering roles: same questions, same scoring rubric, same qualifications for all candidates. Covers the four components (vetted questions, feedback recording, standardized rubrics, interviewer training), behavioral vs hypothetical questions, hiring attribute definition (role knowledge, problem solving, leadership), process design (2-3 rounds, 8-12 day target, score-first discuss-later debrief), bias mitigation, and metrics tracking.

## Key Ideas

- Structured interviews have 2x the predictive validity of unstructured interviews. Unstructured interviews suffer from confirmation bias.
- Google found interviewers save ~40 min per interview with pre-made rubrics, and rejected candidates are 35% happier.
- Four components: vetted questions, comprehensive feedback recording, standardized rubrics (poor/borderline/solid/outstanding), interviewer training and calibration.
- Behavioral questions ("Tell me about a time...") reveal past behavior; hypotheticals ("What would you do if...") test novel-situation thinking. Use both.
- Score-first, discuss-later: everyone writes assessment before debrief to prevent anchoring. Same-day debrief for speed.
- Bias mitigation: standardize questions and rubrics, define culture fit as specific behaviors, diverse panels, track diversity data at drop-off points.
- AI-allowed coding: in 2026, prohibiting AI tools is increasingly unjustifiable — evaluate how candidates use them.
- Common pitfalls: starting without a scorecard, over-indexing on algorithm puzzles, too many rounds, rubrics without interviewer training.
