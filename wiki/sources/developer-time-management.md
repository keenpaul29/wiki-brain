# Developer Time Management and Productivity

## Summary

A guide to developer-specific time management covering Deep Work (Cal Newport's four scheduling philosophies with protection strategies), GTD adapted for developers (capture→clarify→organize→reflect→engage, two-minute rule, weekly review), calendar blocking (maker vs manager schedule, buffer rules, sample block patterns), time-boxing vs modified Pomodoro (45-60 min focus for coding), Slack/email/meeting triage, burnout early signs and prevention, and a 12-item productivity checklist.

## Key Ideas

- Deep Work: complex debugging, architecture, and learning are deep work. 1 hour deep > 5 hours shallow. Four philosophies: monastic, bimodal, rhythmic, journalistic.
- GTD for developers: capture everything, two-minute rule (<2 min = do now), context groups (@computer, @phone, @office), weekly review (30-60 min, pick top 3 MITs).
- Maker vs Manager schedule: developers are makers. Cluster meetings in afternoon, declare no-meeting mornings, replace sync with async.
- Modified Pomodoro for coding: 45-60 min focus + 10 min break (or 90 min ultradian rhythm). Match technique to task — complex coding = 90 min block, simple PR review = 25 min.
- Burnout early signs: hard to get out of bed, dread of Slack, disproportionate anger at small bugs, weekends no longer recover you.
- Prevention: 7-8 hours sleep, 30+ min exercise daily, real lunch break, kill Slack in evening, use all PTO.
- 12-item checklist: 10+ hours/week deep work blocks, one no-meeting day per week, inbox zero weekly, 3 MITs per day, monthly burnout self-check.
- Remote work traps: blurred boundaries, isolation, less movement. Solutions: morning/evening rituals, workspace split, leave house daily.
