# Team Topologies: Engineering Organization Design

## Summary

Team Topologies (Skelton & Pais) is a model for designing team-of-teams organizations where structure follows flow. Covers four fundamental team types (stream-aligned, enabling, complicated-subsystem, platform), three interaction modes (collaboration, X-as-a-Service, facilitation), Conway's Law and the Inverse Conway Maneuver, cognitive load management, and the Thinnest Viable Platform strategy.

## Key Ideas

- Conway's Law: system design mirrors communication structure. Inverse Conway Maneuver deliberately alters team structure to encourage desired software architecture.
- Four team types: Stream-Aligned (full-lifecycle, no hand-offs), Enabling (temporary, builds capability), Complicated-Subsystem (reduces cognitive load on stream teams), Platform (self-service internal product).
- Three interaction modes: Collaboration (high-bandwidth, temporary), X-as-a-Service (low-cost, clear boundaries, default), Facilitation (mentoring without creating dependency).
- Cognitive load: teams can only handle so much complexity. Platform's primary benefit is reducing cognitive load, not standardization or cost.
- Thinnest Viable Platform (TVP): provide just enough capability to accelerate stream-aligned teams without bloat.
- Organizational design is never done — small frequent adjustments beat massive reorganizations.
